# Dujiangyan History & Culture Research — conversation-ready baseline

## Core historical frame

Dujiangyan is not simply a scenic river park. It is a still-functioning ancient hydraulic system built at the Min River's exit from the mountains into the Chengdu Plain. Government and public-water-authority sources describe the project as originating in the late Warring States period under Qin's Shu commandery governor Li Bing, around the 3rd century BCE.

The central engineering idea is **not to block the river with a conventional dam**. Instead, the system works with natural terrain, river curvature, flow velocity and elevation to divide water, discharge floodwater/sediment and control irrigation inflow.

## Three core works

### 1. Yuzui / Fish Mouth — 鱼嘴分水堤

- Divides the Min River into inner and outer channels.
- The inner channel supports irrigation and water supply; the outer channel carries more floodwater and sediment under high-flow conditions.
- It is a good place to explain the philosophy of `因势利导` — guiding according to natural conditions rather than opposing them.

### 2. Feishayan — 飞沙堰

- Functions as an overflow and sediment-discharge element.
- Uses flow dynamics and the curved river course to shed excess water and sediment away from the inner system.
- Useful conversation angle: ancient engineering did not need modern sensors to achieve a form of self-regulating behavior because geometry and flow were doing the work.

### 3. Baopingkou — 宝瓶口

- A narrow artificial intake cut through the mountain ridge.
- Constrains the amount of water entering the Chengdu Plain, functioning as a natural hydraulic bottleneck.
- The separated rock mass is associated with the name `离堆`, which also appears in today's Lidui Park naming.

## Li Bing and the maintenance tradition

The system survived not because it was built once and left untouched, but because a long maintenance tradition developed around it. Famous maintenance principles include `深淘滩，低作堰` and related river-management maxims. They are useful for explaining why Dujiangyan is both an ancient monument and an ongoing infrastructure system.

## Heritage status

- Qingcheng Mountain and the Dujiangyan Irrigation System were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2000.
- The Dujiangyan Irrigation System was also recognized as a World Heritage Irrigation Structure in 2018.
- Modern official sources continue to describe it as a living, large-scale, damless irrigation/flood-control system.

## Cultural / local-atmosphere nodes relevant to a one-day plan

### Erwang Temple / 二王庙

Associated with commemoration of Li Bing and the historical engineering tradition. It is not just a temple stop; it provides a human story behind the infrastructure.

### Anlan Bridge / 安澜索桥

A visually memorable crossing that makes the hydraulic landscape easier to perceive spatially. It is often more useful as a pause-and-observe point than as a check-box photo point.

### Nanqiao / 南桥

A covered bridge and urban-river landmark close to the scenic-area / old-city edge. It works especially well as a transition from daytime heritage touring to evening city atmosphere.

### Guanxian Ancient City / 灌县古城

The old-city street area adds everyday food, street life and urban-history texture that the hydraulic scenic area alone does not provide. For a first-time visitor interested in local atmosphere, pairing the hydraulic system with the old city is stronger than spending the whole day inside the ticketed scenic area.

## Conversation-ready story angles

These are public cultural facts, not private relationship scripts.

1. **Why no giant dam?** Ask what kind of engineering is more impressive: one huge structure or a system that makes geography do the work.
2. **Infrastructure that becomes culture.** Discuss how something originally built for flood control and farming can become a city's identity thousands of years later.
3. **Maintenance vs invention.** The original design mattered, but so did generations of people maintaining and adapting it.
4. **Natural systems thinking.** Fish Mouth, Feishayan and Baopingkou are more interesting when treated as one coordinated system, not three isolated attractions.
5. **Local city relationship.** The old city and riverfront show how the living city grew around the water system rather than the heritage site being isolated from daily life.

## What should not be overstated

- Avoid presenting popular legends as documented engineering history without labels.
- Avoid overly precise ancient construction dates if the source only gives an approximate period.
- Avoid saying the three elements have remained physically unchanged for 2,000+ years; the system has been maintained, repaired and modified through history.

## Planning implication

The final guide should give the traveler enough context **before or while reaching Fish Mouth / Feishayan / Baopingkou** so the site does not feel like merely 'walking beside water'. Recent visitor feedback shows that people who understand the engineering tend to rate the experience much more highly.
