Generate one independent phone-vertical schematic travel route base image for a weather- and crowd-resilient Dujiangyan day trip. The image must clearly distinguish the mandatory core route from optional/cut branches so the traveler can shorten the day safely when rain, slippery surfaces or queues become uncomfortable.

Composition: portrait 9:16. Top-to-bottom rail arrival, compact scenic core, then a prominent decision-point design with solid-line mandatory route and dashed optional branches, followed by central old-city covered/rest-oriented nodes, optional Nanqiao, and rail return. Keep the diagram simple enough to understand at a glance on a phone.

Authoritative base sequence:
Xipu -> Lidui Park station -> Gate 1 -> Baopingkou -> Feishayan -> Yuzui if rain/crowd conditions permit -> skip Anlan Bridge when slippery or heavily queued -> early exit to central old-city side when needed -> covered/indoor-leaning meal/rest placeholder -> short Guanxian Ancient City segment if weather allows -> Nanqiao only if rain/wind/crowd conditions are comfortable -> Lidui Park station -> Xipu.

Operational logic:
- Gate 1 and the hydraulic core are the default stable backbone.
- Yuzui may become conditional under poor conditions; Anlan Bridge is even more conditional.
- do not invent indoor attractions as weather substitutes.
- old-city meal/rest and Nanqiao belong to the same central urban cluster.
- show clear cut points leading directly toward return rather than forcing all optional stops.

Rendering constraints:
- base render only; no readable text, logos, brands, fake roads, fake indoor venues, fake gate names or restaurant names;
- no exact private home-origin point;
- do not merge Lidui Park station and Dujiangyan station;
- no subway into the scenic area;
- leave clean vector-label zones for later verified Chinese labels, weather notes and cut markers.

Visual tone: highly practical contingency travel infographic, calm and reassuring, with obvious solid-vs-optional route hierarchy, not a dramatic weather poster.
