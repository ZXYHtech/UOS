# TASK_INV_AUDIT_MRP_01 — MRP, Shortage Explosion and Replenishment Audit

## 0. Scope

Pinned source: `ZXYHtech/inventory@78d5cda2527cf24836cd5b82a41f02ca8efdd02c`.

Reviewed material/BOM structures, inventory balances, safety stock, purchase orders/receipts, purchase-price history, order demand surfaces and the first four W3 reports covering parametric parts, AVL/substitution, revision control and EBOM/MBOM.

This is an analysis-only report. It does not modify the external inventory repository.

## 1. Executive conclusion

The current application has enough raw data to produce **basic low-stock warnings**, but not enough semantics to run trustworthy manufacturing MRP.

Existing strengths:

- item-level safety stock exists;
- current inventory can be queried;
- purchase orders and partial receipts exist;
- BOM recursion exists for costing/fulfilment;
- customer orders exist;
- supplier purchase history exists.

Missing planning foundations:

- authoritative reservations/allocations;
- time-phased supply and demand;
- released effective MBOM revision;
- work orders and WIP supply/demand;
- lead-time/MOQ/order-multiple supplier sourcing model;
- lot/quality state exclusions;
- planned engineering/sample demand;
- yield/scrap factors;
- planning policies by material/site;
- pegging and exception messages.

Therefore a naive formula such as `safety_stock - quantity_available` must **not** be branded MRP. Preliminary MRP maturity: **1.0/5**.

## 2. Critical planning distinction

MRP must distinguish:

```text
On-hand != Available != Nettable supply != ATP
```

A robust planning calculation needs at least:

```text
gross requirement
- qualified/nettable on-hand
- firm incoming supply
+ safety stock / policy requirement
= net requirement
```

Then it must place that requirement in time using due dates and lead times.

Current balances are not sufficient because engineering stock, quarantine, reservations, in-transit and other states are not consistently authoritative.

## 3. Demand sources

Future MRP should ingest typed demand rather than scan unrelated tables and infer intent.

Recommended demand classes:

1. released production/work orders;
2. planned production orders;
3. firm customer orders for make-to-order items;
4. forecast demand where policy permits;
5. prototype/project demand;
6. service/RMA demand;
7. safety-stock target replenishment;
8. dependent demand generated by MBOM explosion.

Each demand record needs:

- material/site;
- required quantity;
- need date;
- priority/class;
- source document;
- firm/planned status;
- cancellation/change trace.

## 4. Supply sources

At minimum:

- qualified on-hand inventory;
- approved open purchase-order balance;
- released/planned work-order receipts;
- inter-warehouse transfer receipts;
- subcontract return supply;
- optionally confirmed supplier schedule lines.

Do not count these as usable supply by default:

- quarantine stock;
- failed/NCR stock;
- engineering/golden samples;
- customer-owned or consigned stock belonging to another owner;
- expired/restricted lot;
- unapproved substitute;
- draft PO/WO.

## 5. Time-phased netting

MRP should operate in planning buckets/dates, not one total number.

For each material/site, calculate projected available balance across time:

```text
PAB(t) = PAB(t-1)
         + scheduled_receipts(t)
         + planned_receipts(t)
         - gross_requirements(t)
```

When the projected balance violates safety stock or reaches shortage, produce a planned order or exception.

The initial implementation can use daily buckets for a small company. Avoid prematurely implementing complex enterprise planning calendars.

## 6. BOM explosion requirements

Production dependent demand must use the **released effective MBOM revision**, as established by `TASK_INV_AUDIT_EBOM_MBOM_01`.

Explosion requirements:

- multi-level recursion;
- cycle detection;
- revision/effective-date selection;
- quantity propagation;
- scrap/yield factor;
- phantom assembly handling;
- fixed-quantity vs per-unit components when needed;
- optional/DNF/variant applicability;
- units of measure;
- substitute resolution as a separate controlled decision.

Do not explode mutable draft engineering BOMs into procurement recommendations.

## 7. Reservations and allocation dependency

MRP is blocked from being trustworthy until stock reservations are authoritative.

The existing inventory schema exposes locked/on-transfer quantities, but the core service code does not show an authoritative use of those quantities for all demand flows.

Target supply model should expose, per material/site:

```text
physical_on_hand
reserved_sales
reserved_work_order
reserved_project
quarantine
engineering_non_saleable
in_transfer
nettable_on_hand
ATP
```

These can be derived from a reservation/state ledger rather than permanently duplicated columns, but there must be one authoritative calculation.

## 8. Procurement planning data

The current supplier and purchase history are useful but not enough for sourcing recommendations.

Add source-policy data such as:

```text
item_supplier_source
  internal_part_id
  supplier_part_id
  site_id
  approved
  preferred_rank
  lead_time_days
  min_order_qty
  order_multiple
  price_break/reference
  currency
  valid_from/to
  supplier_capacity_or_limit (optional)
```

Planning should prefer approved sources and surface an exception if the required part has no released/approved source.

For electronics, MPN/AVL/alternate policy is part of MRP input integrity, not merely purchasing UI metadata.

## 9. Planning policies

Minimum per-item/site settings:

- make / buy / transfer;
- safety stock;
- reorder point where used;
- lot sizing: lot-for-lot initially;
- MOQ/order multiple;
- purchase lead time;
- manufacturing lead time;
- preferred warehouse/site;
- planning time fence (later);
- whether forecast and customer demand consume each other (later).

Do not overload `materials.safety_stock` with all planning policy.

## 10. Planned-order output

MRP should create **recommendations first**, not silently create executable POs or WOs.

Suggested lifecycle:

```text
MRP run
 -> exception / recommendation
 -> planner review
 -> firm planned order
 -> approved purchase order or work order
```

Every recommendation should explain itself:

- why quantity is needed;
- demand sources/pegging;
- current nettable supply;
- expected receipts;
- selected BOM revision;
- source/lead-time assumptions;
- suggested release and due dates.

This explainability is essential for planner trust.

## 11. Pegging

For shortages, the user should be able to drill from a component back to consuming demand.

Example:

```text
AD8318 shortage: 12 pcs
  -> WO-2026-0107: 8
      -> Customer order SO-...
  -> Prototype PB-2026-021: 4
```

Pegging need not be perfect enterprise-level dynamic pegging in v1, but source-document links should be preserved through explosion.

## 12. Exception messages

High-value exceptions:

- shortage by need date;
- planned order release overdue;
- PO receipt late relative to demand;
- excess supply after demand cancellation;
- missing approved supplier/AVL;
- invalid/unreleased BOM revision;
- substitute requires engineering approval;
- MOQ causes excess;
- safety stock violation;
- long-lead part beyond planning horizon;
- purchase/work-order date conflict.

The dashboard should prioritize exceptions requiring action instead of only showing aggregate inventory value.

## 14. Scenario for an RF-module company

A useful acceptance scenario:

- customer demand requires 20 finished RF modules in two weeks;
- released MBOM consumes LNA, SAW filter, PCB, SMA connectors and shield;
- five finished units exist;
- a prototype project has reserved four SAW filters;
- one PO is due for ten SAW filters;
- two filters are quarantined after IQC;
- the preferred supplier has 14-day lead time and MOQ 20;
- an approved alternate exists but requires a specific product-revision applicability rule.

MRP must show the actual shortage by date, not simply subtract total warehouse quantity.

## 15. Priority roadmap

4. supplier schedule/capacity constraints;
5. simulation/scenario planning.

## 16. Acceptance signals

- the same physical quantity cannot satisfy both a sales reservation and a production requirement;
- MRP excludes quarantine and engineering non-saleable stock;
- a released MBOM revision and effective date are recorded for every exploded demand;
- open PO remaining quantity is recognized on its expected date;
- MOQ/order multiple are respected and excess is explained;
- every suggested buy/make/transfer action can be traced to dated demand;
- cancelling demand causes an explainable reschedule/cancel/excess exception;
- no executable PO/WO is silently generated without planner control;
- no acceptance test requires GitHub Actions.

## 17. Core recommendation

Do **not** start by building a large MRP screen. First establish one authoritative planning equation over typed stock states, dated demand/supply and released MBOMs. Once those contracts exist, the UI and planning automation become comparatively straightforward.
